How it works

From sign-up to your customers using your components, without running a server of your own.

  1. 1 Register

    Sign in with GitHub, Google or your company's login. You get a workspace and an MCP address your customers can connect to. 

    https://api.app-mcp.com
  /w/<workspace>/mcp

  2. 2 Add your components

    Write them with @app-mcp/kit in TypeScript or the framework you use, with workflows in Markdown next to them. Props are a Zod schema; the build is one HTML file. 

    defineComponent({
  name: "rating",
  props: z.object({ … }),
});

  3. 3 Test them

    Publish, add your address as a connector in Claude or ChatGPT, and try the flow as your customers will. Change it and publish again. 

    $ app-mcp publish
# serves rating 1.3.0

  4. 4 Your customers use it

    Share the address. Customers add it as a connector, and new versions reach running chats without a restart. Need it to act on your systems? Connect your data. 

    Settings → Connectors → Add
https://api.app-mcp.com
  /w/<workspace>/mcp

Get started

Everything runs through two connectors: app-mcp's, where you manage your workspace, and your workspace's own, the one your customers add. Custom connectors need a paid Claude plan or ChatGPT's developer mode.

app-mcp's connector https://api.app-mcp.com/mcp

  1. 1 Add app-mcp and sign in

    In Claude: Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector, with the address above. Connect, then sign in with Google or GitHub. You get a workspace. 

    Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector
https://api.app-mcp.com/mcp

  2. 2 Try the built-ins

    Ask Claude to open your app-mcp workspace. It shows your workspace's MCP address: add it as a second connector, then ask for a form or a chart. 

    "Open my app-mcp workspace"
→ https://api.app-mcp.com/w/<workspace>/mcp

  3. 3 Build your component

    Start one with app-mcp init, then try it in a local host with live props and theme. It's TypeScript with @app-mcp/kit; use any framework you like. 

    $ npx @app-mcp/cli init my_poll
$ cd my_poll && npm install
$ npm run dev

  4. 4 Publish it

    Create a publish token in the workspace view and publish. Your workspace's connector now offers render_my_poll. Remove components or turn off the built-in forms and charts under Manage components. 

    $ APP_MCP_TOKEN=gnu_publish_… npm run app-mcp:publish
# Published my_poll@0.1.0 to prod

What you can build

Whatever your customers' flows need. app-mcp handles the plumbing around it, so your team writes the component.

Your components
A seat picker, a quote builder, a rating: anything your flow needs. Plain TypeScript or the framework you use, with props as a Zod schema.
defineComponent({ … })
Workflows
Playbooks in your own words, kept next to your components and published with them. A workflow with params is a slash command; one without is guidance the model always has.
/create_order
Forms and charts, built in
For everything you haven't built yet. Text, numbers, dates, switches and selects; line, bar, area and pie charts.
render_form, render_chart
Your theme
Views follow the host's colors, fonts and dark mode. Set your primary color, accent, corner radius and chart colors for each environment, with a live preview in the chat.
--app-mcp-primary
Environments
Add staging or any other environment when you want one. Each has its own address, versions, theme and workflows, so testers can keep it next to prod in the same chat. Publish a tested version to prod without rebuilding it.
app-mcp publish --env staging
Loading states
Fields appear while the model is still writing. Submits can't fire twice, and failures tell the model what went wrong.
streaming → ready

Need your own data? Connect your MCP server

When a flow needs your customers, orders or prices, mount app-mcp in the MCP server you already run. Your data stays on your server.

One call in your server

Your components, forms and charts become tools on your server, next to the ones you have, and your workflows become its slash commands. 

await mountAppMcp(server, {
  appMcp: connectAppMcp({
    url: "…/w/<workspace>/mcp",
    token,
  }),
});

Views that use your tools

Selects search your tools as people type, charts load rows from your tools so private data stays out of the model's context, and submit errors from your tool show next to the field. 

{
  "name": "customer",
  "type": "resource_select",
  "source": {
    "toolName": "search_customers"
  }
}

Calls to app-mcp's tools

app-mcp

Serves forms, charts, workflows and your components. It sees calls to its own tools, never your tools' calls or their results.

  • Versions that never change

    A published version is immutable and its bundle is kept, so old messages keep rendering the exact version they were made with.

  • People confirm, not the model

    Saving the theme, instructions or workflows is hidden from the model. It runs only from a click in the view.

Nothing may leave your network? Run app-mcp yourself

The same registry as one container with a SQLite file, inside your network. People sign in with GitHub, Google or your company's OIDC provider, and everything above works the same.
